# The story layer

Developer notes moved out of CLAUDE.md. Read when touching anything under src/career/story/, src/service/story.rs, the Paddock, headlines, templates, the title fight, rival, targets, mood, interest, the season review or the career biography.

## A season's story (`src/career/story.rs`, `src/service/story.rs`)

The story and management layer, built in phases — `plans/story-and-management.md` is the plan and its ground rules. Phases 1 (the teammate head-to-head), 2 (the news feed), 3 (the title fight), 4 (the rival), 5 (weekend targets), 6 (team mood), 7a (team lineage), 7 (mood at renewal), 8 (interest from other teams) and 9 (season review and career biography) — the whole plan — exist.

- **Worked out, never stored.** Like the rating and the offers, every part is derived from the season's results on each request. Only a player's *choice* is ever stored — so far only the team lineage (`Championship::team_lineage`). No AI/LLM writes any of it: every sentence is a template filled with career facts.
- **`SeasonFacts` is the one input** (`story/facts.rs`). The service builds it — the roster read through `champ_roster_seats`, so a signed season uses its copy and phantom entries are gone — and every story function takes `&SeasonFacts`. Story modules never import a roster reader, the config or the store, which is why `tests/career/story/teammate.rs` builds facts by hand. Keep it that way: a rule that needs something new gets a field on the facts, filled by the service.
- What the facts settle once: practice is dropped (nothing is derived from it); qualifying sorts before the race within a round; empty rounds are kept so `RoundFacts::number` matches the tabs; each result is matched to its roster entry by `custom_ai::name_key` — the same match seat inference uses — with a regular entry winning over a stand-in of the same name; race points come from `data_store::race_points`, which is now also what the standings score with.
- **Dependencies point one way.** Story reads the career; nothing outside `story` and `service/story` reads story. When a later phase needs to feed something back (mood into renewals, sponsor money into the ledger), the service passes a plain value in — `contracts` must never import `story`.
- **A teammate** is a regular roster driver of `player_team`: never a per-track stand-in, never the driver `player_seat.replaces` (the player is in that car), and one entry per name however many liveries. Alternates sharing a seat are each a teammate. Only sessions both drove count; in a race a classified finish beats a retirement and two retirements count for nobody.
- Two facts fields come from outside the season and are the service's to fill after `SeasonFacts::new`: `history` (the player's earlier seasons — every other championship whose first counted session was recorded before this one's, folded by `PlayerHistory::before`) and `expected` (each team's expected position from `champ_eligibility`; empty in MP or without a roster). `SessionFacts::track` is the name a sentence says — no layout, underscores as spaces.
- `GET /api/championships/:id/story` answers `SeasonStory`, one field per part (`teammates`, `news`). Each part carries a `reason` instead of being empty, so a card says *why* it has nothing.

**The news feed (`story/headlines.rs`, `headlines/player.rs`, `headlines/field.rs`)**

- **One kind of headline is one `HeadlineRule` in `RULES`**; adding one is a struct and a line there. The walker (`for_season_with`) goes through the rounds keeping a `SeasonSoFar` tally (`story/tally.rs` — its own module because the title maths reads it too), hands each rule a `RoundContext` with the tally **before and after** the round (a lead change needs both), and never matches on a kind. A rule needing a new figure adds it to the tally, not to the walker.
- **A rule never holds a sentence.** It returns a `HeadlineDraft` — a template key plus values — and `story/templates.rs` words it. Every sentence the app can write is in that table, by key; a key with several variants is picked by a seed of champ id + session id + key + subject through `career::seed::hash64`, so the same career always reads the same and a reload never rerolls. No AI writes any of it. `test_every_placeholder_is_one_a_rule_fills` catches a misspelt `{placeholder}`, which would otherwise print its braces. The teammate summary is worded through the same table.
- Caps per round: `MAX_PER_ROUND` (4) by priority, then rule order, and `MAX_PER_SUBJECT` (2) so a weekend's winner does not take every slot. Priorities are on the rules, 0–100.
- **"First" means first of the career where the history can say so** (`win.maiden`, `podium.maiden` — a win counts as a podium, so no "first podium" after a win — `pole.maiden`, first points), and first of the season otherwise (first retirement). A debut is the first race of a career or the first for a team other than `history.last_team`.
- Player rules use the round's last race (`RoundFacts::race`) and last qualifying; field rules skip the player, whose wins and leads are the player rules' news. A **tie on points is no leader** (`SeasonSoFar::leader`): the feed does not announce a lead only the countback decides. An upset needs `expected`, so a season with no roster has none.
- Worked out on every request: reassigning a session rewrites the feed, by design.

**The title fight (`story/title.rs`, `headlines/title.rs`)**

- **It needs the calendar.** "What is still to race for" has no answer without `planned_rounds`, so a season without one gets no fight and a `reason` saying to set Races. That is why the Races box is now shown in **every** mode (Manage tab and the create form) — still required only in singleplayer, where the salary is split across it. Races run = rounds holding a race (the salary's own test); races left saturate at 0, since a season may run past its plan. Each race left is worth the points table's best score — there are no pole or fastest-lap points to add.
- **Ranked through the standings' own comparator.** `data_store::standing_order` (points → countback → name) is what `rank` and `SeasonSoFar::table` both sort with, and `data_store::Countback` is `pub(crate)` for it. Do not write a second ordering.
- **Clinched is never claimed on a tie that could go the other way.** A rival is out only if they cannot finish ahead even by winning every race left while the leader scores nothing; level on points, their countback *with those wins added* must be strictly worse. An exact countback tie keeps both in the fight — only the name would separate them, and that is not an FIA rule. The leader has clinched exactly when every rival is out.
- `clinch_next`: the leader can settle it at the next round when winning it with everyone else scoring nothing would leave nobody able to catch up. The margins it gives are strict (ahead on points alone), and if only the countback made it possible the sentence is dropped rather than promising something a countback might take away.
- The objective is the contract's `objective` (a championship position, met at or above it — the ledger's own test), read off the contract by the service into `SeasonFacts::objective`, never recomputed. The tracker gives the position now and the points to whoever holds the target place — not "the average finish needed", which would depend on everyone else's results.
- Two headlines compare the fight before and after a round, so each fires once: `TitleClinched` (anyone, 98) and `PlayerOutOfTheFight` (55).
- `TitleView.summary` ("8 of 12 races run — 14 points behind Nelson Piquet") is also the **Live tab's** line for the `Active` season (`#live-title-line`, `loadLiveTitle` in `story.js`), asked for at page load and whenever the Live tab is opened. `past_plan` tells an open season whose planned races are all run to finish it — never a `Final` one.

**The rival (`story/rival.rs`, `headlines/rival.rs`)**

- **Chosen by the season, never stored.** `Rivalry` lives in the tally and is replayed with it: each race the player started, every other driver's weight decays by `DECAY` (0.75, so recent races count most) and gains proximity (3/2/1 for finishing 1/2/3 places from the player, nothing when either retired) plus standings closeness (`STANDINGS_WEIGHT` level on points, fading to nothing two races' worth of points apart). A race the player missed changes nothing.
- **Hysteresis is what makes it a rival rather than last week's neighbour.** Nobody is named before `MIN_RACES` (2); after that the heaviest becomes the rival, and a challenger takes over only after out-weighing them by `SWITCH_MARGIN` for `SWITCH_RACES` (2) races running. Ties fall to the name, so the answer never depends on map order. The constants sit at the top of the module; the reference career settles a rival at round 2 and changes it once or twice a season.
- Teammates (`teammate::teammates`) are never the rival: they have their own card, and the car makes them close by default. In multiplayer the rival can be a person.
- `Duel` is the head-to-head over every race both started, counted through `DriverResult::beats` — the one finish-beats-retirement rule, which the teammate card now uses too.
- Headlines: `NewRival` (`rival.new`, or `rival.replaced` naming who was replaced) and `RivalDuelTurns` (the head-to-head with the *same* rival changing hands). The `RivalView` sentence and the card sit on the Paddock after the weekend targets.

**Weekend targets (`story/targets.rs`)**

- **One kind of target is one `TargetKind` in `TARGETS`**: `set` (does it fit this weekend, and with what) and `judge` (`Met` / `Missed` / `Void`). `season_with` sets each raced round's target from the tally **before** that round, judges it on the round's race (the last race of a weekend), and sets `next` for the first unraced round after the last race. One kind is picked per round, by `hash64(champ, "target", round)` over the kinds that fit — so a fair target always, varied, never rerolled.
- `TopN` asks for the car's expected place (`SeasonFacts::expected`, rounded up) plus `TARGET_SLACK` (2), and only when that still leaves cars behind — the field is the last race's grid, or the roster's non-stand-in drivers before the first. `BeatTeammate` fits whenever the team runs another car and is void when no teammate started. `BringItHome` fits only straight after a retirement.
- Needs a team and a roster (empty otherwise; the view says why), so the card is singleplayer/unset only and `story.js` leaves it out in multiplayer. **No money and no rating** hang off a target: the team-mood phase is what reads the verdicts. `TargetsView.next` is also the Live tab's second line (`#live-target-line`), filled with the title line by `loadLiveTitle`.

**Team mood (`story/mood.rs`, `headlines/mood.rs`)**

- **One influence is one `MoodFactor` in `FACTORS`**, its weight a named constant beside it. `replay_with` walks the races the player started: the mood first eases back toward `START` (60) by `REVERSION` (10% of the distance), then each factor adds its delta, clamped 0–100. A race the player missed changes nothing. Bands: Delighted ≥ 75, Content ≥ 55, Concerned ≥ 35, else Losing patience.
- Factors: `WeekendTarget` (±6, the target's verdict, void counts for nothing), `CarPerformance` (0.25 per place gained on the car's expected finish, +5 / −4 at most), `TeammateDuel` (±4, averaged over the teammates who started), `ObjectivePace` (+2 on course for the contract's objective, −1 short, −3 more than a race's points short) and `Retirement` (−2 — small, because nothing says whether it was the driver or the engine).
- **`CarPerformance` is the one every weekend has**, and it was added when tuning against the real 1983 save: a one-car team has no teammate, a back-marker gets no top-N target, and that season's contract set no objective, so without it a driver hauling the slowest car to a win barely moved the team. The car's expected finish is **capped at the grid's size** — the 1983 roster expects Spirit-Honda 26th on a 24-car grid, and without the cap every finish would look like a giant-killing.
- **Tuned on copies of real saves, never the originals** (`F:\google_drive\ams2_champs`, copied to the scratchpad). 1983 (Spirit-Honda, a win and several top-tens in the slowest car) ends Delighted at 85 after an early dip to Concerned; 1969 (Brabham-Repco) climbs to Delighted after two met targets and drops back to Content after a P24.
- `MoodShift` headlines the round the mood crosses into another band, worded by direction and band (`mood.up.delighted` … `mood.down.losing_patience`). It replays the season for each round it checks — a few dozen races at most.
- No rating reads it. **Money does, once, at the next renewal** — see *Mood at renewal* below. `MoodView` carries the figure, band, what the team says, why it last moved (every factor that touched the last race, beside the net change — the drift is part of the change but never listed), a per-race trend the card draws as plain CSS bars, and `warning` once the team has been losing patience for `WARNING_RACES` (3) races running. `ConsideringOptions` headlines that third race, once per run. **It is a warning only: nobody is sacked**, a contract runs for its season. Singleplayer/unset with a team and a roster only; left out in multiplayer.

**Mood at renewal (`contracts::TeamMood`, `contracts::renews`)**

- The service hands the contracts the mood the team **ended the last completed season** with, as a plain `TeamMood` on `Standing::mood` (`lineage::carried` fills it via `App::season_end_mood`). `contracts` never imports `story`; `Standing::default()` and every path without a mood carry `None`, which renews exactly as before — the reference tests are unchanged.
- `renews(delivered, mood)`: a met target always renews (delivering keeps the seat, whatever the mood); a missed target renews only if the team is **delighted**; with no target set (a back-marker's deal) the team renews unless it is **losing patience**. Never worse than merit: a dropped renewal leaves the team's ordinary offer if the rating earns one.
- A delighted renewal adds `DELIGHTED_BONUS` (0.10 of the rate) on top of leverage and loyalty — smaller than either, because it is one season's feeling.
- The offer sentence says when mood decided it ("you missed the target, but they are delighted enough with you to keep you", "…with a little extra because they are delighted with you"), and the summary adds how the team ended the season.

**Interest from other teams (`App::interest_view`)**

- From two thirds of the calendar (`INTEREST_FROM`), the teams **on this season's grid** whose offer the player would get today: `offers_for_with` on the current rating and grid, as a newcomer (`Standing::default()` — interest, not renewal), `Paid` offers only (a team that would sell the seat is interested in the money, not the driver), the player's own team left out, each flagged when it is a quicker car. The card says plainly it is this grid: next season's offers are made on next season's roster when that season is created — the app cannot see that grid mid-season.
- `season_story` works out the rating **once** (`champ_eligibility`) and hands it to both the facts' expected positions (`season_facts_rated`) and the interest card. No interest headlines: they would need the rating replayed race by race, which is the dearest computation in the app.

**Season review and career biography (`story/review.rs`, `story/biography.rs`)**

- `review` gathers what a finished season came to from the other modules — champion and the player's place (tally), objective (title), best and worst weekend (first time the best finish came; the first retirement, else the lowest finish), teammate and rival, the mood arc, and the five highest-priority headlines in round order. `ReviewView` words it; it is empty with a `reason` until the season is `Final`.
- `biography` orders seasons by their first recorded session (a championship has no date of its own) and skips one with nothing recorded; a line per season (team, place or *Champion*, points, wins) and the career's firsts (start, points, podium, win, pole, title — a title only from a `Final` season). `GET /api/career/story` (`App::career_story`) reads only the player's results and team, so it builds facts with no roster and no rating — cheap for any number of seasons. `latest_finished` names the last finished season raced.
- Shown: the review on the Championships sub-tab for a `Final` season (`#champ-review`, `loadChampReview`); "Your career" at the foot of the Paddock's left column (`loadPaddockCareer`, fetched separately so the season's cards do not wait for it); and **between seasons the Paddock shows the last finished season** — its review, the career and its news — instead of an empty page. The HTML export opens with a Career section and puts each finished season's review above its standings (`career_story_block`, `review_block` in `service/export.rs`).
- **The story has its own page: Career → Paddock** (`#career-sub-paddock`), not the Manage tab, which is for setting a season up. It shows the `Active` season and only that one — **no season picker, on purpose**: singleplayer races one season at a time, the Paddock is the story still being written, and a finished season's results already live on the Championships sub-tab, with its review. With nothing `Active` it shows the last finished season's review instead. The season comes from `/api/championships`, so it does not depend on the Championships sub-tab having loaded. Every later story part is a card on this page. **Layout:** two columns on a wide screen — `.paddock-side` stacks the short cards (title fight, team mood, weekend targets, interest, rival, teammate, your career), `.paddock-news` holds the feed, which scrolls inside its own card with a sticky heading so the page itself barely scrolls; one column below 1100px. A new long, growing card belongs on the right, a new status card on the left. It is shown in multiplayer too, since the news needs no team; `story.js` leaves the teammate card out there.
